# TREATMENT — "The Line"

Style: **3D Map Explainer** (`map-explainer`). 1920×1080, 24 fps, ~60 s, English narration (Kokoro, offline), burned-in subtitles. Facts: `FACTS.md`.

## 1. Three structures

1. **Funnel** (globe → Iran → bases → back out). Clear, but it is the style's default and the demo's shape; the news (the limit is gone) would arrive late.
2. **Before / after on one terrain.** The 2,000 km line glows around Iran, a missile path crosses it, the line breaks; then the camera climbs to show the real distance that remains. The news *is* the turn.
3. **Countdown of distances** (2,000 → 4,000 → 5,500 → 10,177 km), each a pillar on the ground. Strong numbers, but a list, no turn.

**Chosen: 2.** The film is about a line on a map, so the line is the subject: it is drawn, it is crossed, it is erased, and what is left is the distance. Opening: a single glowing point on a dark region (not the demo's dive from orbit). Ending: the last quantity (10,177 km) held over the Atlantic as the camera settles (not a cross-section, no pillars).

## 2. Logline and arc

*Iran erased the line around its missiles; the film measures what that line was, what crossed it, and what is still out of reach.*

- **Setup**: one point (Tehran) in a dark region; the 2,000 km line ignites along its true course; what lies inside lights up (Israel, the Gulf, five US bases).
- **Turn**: a path grows south over the Arabian Sea toward Diego Garcia, past the line (≈4,000 km). One path dies short, one ends in an interception spark. Then the ring breaks apart: "limit lifted".
- **Ending**: the camera climbs out to the globe, a warm-white measured line runs from Tehran to Washington: 10,177 km; a tick at 5,500 km marks where "intercontinental" begins; "2035 — if it decides to". The line is gone; the distance isn't.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Learn**: newsroom 3D terrain explainers (one claim per move, labels where things are, measurement lines that are drawn then labelled); *Powers of Ten* (a continuous change of scale as the argument).
- **Don't take**: any newsroom's colours, icons, typefaces, camera paths; no logos; no footage.
