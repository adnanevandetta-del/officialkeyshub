Microsoft ended support for Windows 10 in October 2025, and its consumer security updates (ESU) end in **October 2027**. Sooner or later you'll move to Windows 11. The good news: for most people it costs **nothing**, and for everyone else it should cost far less than Microsoft's $139–199 list price.

## Step 1: check if your PC can run Windows 11

Windows 11 needs:

| Requirement | Minimum |
| --- | --- |
| CPU | Intel 8th gen / AMD Ryzen 2000 or newer, 64-bit |
| RAM | 4 GB |
| Storage | 64 GB |
| Security chip | TPM 2.0 |
| Firmware | UEFI with Secure Boot |

The quick way to check: install Microsoft's free **PC Health Check** app and click **Check now**. It tells you exactly which requirement fails, if any.

**TPM "not found"?** On most PCs from 2018 onwards it's just switched off. Restart into BIOS/UEFI and enable **Intel PTT** or **AMD fTPM**, then turn on **Secure Boot**. Run PC Health Check again.

## Step 2: back up first

Upgrading keeps your files and apps, but power cuts and failed updates happen. Copy your Documents, Desktop and Pictures to OneDrive or an external drive before you start. Five minutes now, zero regrets later.

## Step 3: the free upgrade (most people)

If Windows 10 is **activated** (Settings → Update & Security → Activation) and your PC passes the check:

1. Open **Settings → Update & Security → Windows Update**.
2. If you see **"Upgrade to Windows 11 is ready"**, click **Download and install**.
3. Not showing? Use Microsoft's **Windows 11 Installation Assistant** from the [Windows 11 download page](https://www.microsoft.com/software-download/windows11).

Your licence moves across automatically: **Windows 10 Home → Windows 11 Home**, **Windows 10 Pro → Windows 11 Pro**. No key needed.

## When you *do* need a Windows 11 key

You'll need to buy a licence if:

- **Windows 10 was never activated** (the "Activate Windows" watermark).
- You're building a **new PC** or putting in a **new blank drive**.
- You replaced the **motherboard** on a PC with an OEM licence, which is tied to the original board.
- You want to go from **Home to Pro**.

This is where most people overpay. Microsoft sells Windows 11 Pro for **$199.99**. A genuine key here is **$16.99** ([Windows 11 Pro](/products/windows-11-pro)) or **$12.99** ([Windows 11 Home](/products/windows-11-home)).

Not sure which edition? [Windows 11 Home vs Pro](/blog/windows-11-home-vs-pro) breaks it down. Short version: Pro adds BitLocker, Remote Desktop hosting, Hyper-V and Group Policy. For about $4 more, most people take Pro.

## Upgrading Home to Pro (no reinstall)

Already on Windows 11 Home and want Pro?

1. Open **Settings → System → Activation**.
2. Click **Change product key** next to *Upgrade your edition of Windows*.
3. Enter your Windows 11 Pro key.
4. Windows installs the Pro features and restarts. Your files and apps stay put.

The same works on Windows 10 Home → Pro.

## Clean install on a new PC or drive

1. On any working PC, download the **Media Creation Tool** from Microsoft and make a bootable USB (8 GB or bigger).
2. Boot the new PC from the USB.
3. Enter your key when asked, or click **"I don't have a product key"** and activate later in Settings.
4. Choose the edition that matches your key (Home or Pro). Picking the wrong one is the #1 cause of "this key doesn't work".

Setting up the whole machine? The [Windows 11 Pro + Office 2024 bundle](/products/windows-11-pro-office-2024-pro-plus-bundle) is $44.99 for both.

## What about unsupported PCs?

There are registry tweaks and tools that install Windows 11 on older CPUs. They work, but Microsoft says unsupported PCs **aren't guaranteed updates**, and a big feature update can refuse to install. If your PC is too old, your realistic options are:

- Stay on Windows 10 with **ESU** until October 2027 (see [Windows 10 end of support](/blog/windows-10-end-of-support-2026), and the full [Microsoft end of support dates](/microsoft-end-of-support) table).
- Put Windows 11 on a newer, cheap refurbished PC, and move your licence if it's retail ([how to transfer a Windows licence](/blog/transfer-windows-license-new-pc)).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Windows 10 to Windows 11 upgrade still free in 2026?

Yes. An activated Windows 10 PC that meets Windows 11's hardware requirements can still upgrade for free through Windows Update or the Installation Assistant.

### Will I lose my files upgrading to Windows 11?

No. The in-place upgrade keeps your files, apps and settings. Back up anyway in case something goes wrong mid-install.

### Does my Windows 10 key work for Windows 11?

An activated Windows 10 licence carries over when you upgrade, and Windows 10 Home and Pro keys generally activate the matching Windows 11 edition on a clean install too.

### Can I go back to Windows 10 after upgrading?

Yes, within 10 days of upgrading. Go to Settings → System → Recovery → Go back. After 10 days, the old files are removed.

### How much does it cost to upgrade Windows 11 Home to Pro?

Microsoft charges $99 for the Home-to-Pro upgrade in the Microsoft Store. A standalone Windows 11 Pro key from a key store costs much less and upgrades Home in place through Settings → System → Activation.

### Is there a deadline for the free Windows 11 upgrade?

Microsoft hasn't announced an end date. Eligible, activated Windows 10 PCs that are up to date can still upgrade for free through Windows Update. If your PC isn't offered it, use the Installation Assistant.
