Going from **Windows 11 Home to Pro** doesn't need a reinstall. You enter a Pro product key, Windows unlocks the Pro features and restarts, and everything else stays as it was. Here's how, plus what it costs and what to do if it fails.

## TL;DR

**Settings → System → Activation → Upgrade your edition of Windows → Change product key**, enter your Windows 11 Pro key, and restart. Your files, apps and settings stay.

## What you get with Pro

Pro adds the features Home leaves out, mostly for work and security:

- **BitLocker** drive encryption.
- **Remote Desktop**, so you can connect to this PC from another one.
- **Hyper-V** virtual machines and **Windows Sandbox**.
- **Group Policy** and joining a work **domain** or Azure AD.
- More control over Windows Update.

If you're not sure you need these, read [Windows 11 Home vs Pro](/blog/windows-11-home-vs-pro) first.

## Before you start

- Make sure **Windows 11 Home is activated**. Check in Settings → System → Activation.
- Save your open work: the upgrade ends with a restart.
- Have your **Windows 11 Pro** key ready. A Windows 10 Pro key also works on Windows 11.

## Step by step

1. Open **Settings** (Windows key + I).
2. Go to **System → Activation**.
3. Expand **Upgrade your edition of Windows**.
4. Next to **Change product key**, click **Change**.
5. Enter your 25-character **Pro key** and click **Next**.
6. Click **Start** when Windows offers to upgrade to Pro.

Windows prepares the upgrade, restarts once or twice, and comes back as **Windows 11 Pro**, already activated. It usually takes 5 to 15 minutes.

## How much does it cost?

There are two ways to pay:

- **Microsoft Store**: the "Upgrade to Pro" button in Settings sells the upgrade directly, about **$99** in the US.
- **A Pro product key**: enter it in the steps above. Keys cost much less, and ours is [Windows 11 Pro for $16.99](/products/windows-11-pro), emailed after payment with a 30-day money-back guarantee.

Both end the same way: a Pro licence for that PC.

## If the upgrade fails

**"This key doesn't work"** or error **0x800F0805**: Windows sometimes refuses to switch editions directly. The usual fix is a two-step upgrade:

1. In **Change product key**, enter Microsoft's default Windows Pro setup key: `VK7JG-NPHTM-C97JM-9MPGT-3V66T`. This key only **switches the edition to Pro**. It does not activate anything.
2. Let Windows upgrade and restart.
3. Go back to **Settings → System → Activation → Change product key** and enter **your own Pro key** to activate.

Other errors:

- **"Windows can't activate"** after the upgrade: check your internet connection and run the [activation steps](/blog/how-to-activate-windows-11) again.
- **The key is blocked** or "already in use": see [Windows product key blocked](/blog/windows-product-key-blocked) and contact your seller.
- **Your PC runs Windows 11 in S mode**: switch out of S mode first (free, from the Microsoft Store), then upgrade.

## Does the upgrade stay if I reinstall?

Yes. Once Pro is activated, Windows links a **digital licence** to your hardware, and to your Microsoft account if you're signed in. A clean reinstall of Windows 11 Pro on the same PC activates again automatically. More in [digital licence vs product key](/blog/digital-license-vs-product-key).

## FAQ

### Will I lose my files upgrading from Home to Pro?

No. It's an in-place edition upgrade. Files, apps and settings stay. A backup is still a good habit before any system change.

### Can I use a Windows 10 Pro key to upgrade Windows 11 Home?

Yes. Windows 10 Pro keys activate Windows 11 Pro.

### Can I go back from Pro to Home?

Not by changing the key. You'd need to reinstall Windows 11 Home. Most people never need to: Pro does everything Home does.

### Do I need to reinstall Windows to get Pro?

No. Entering a Pro key upgrades the installed system in place.
