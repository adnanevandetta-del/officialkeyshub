You're reinstalling Windows, or moving to a new SSD, and Windows asks for a product key. Cool. Where is it? 💀

Here's every legit place your key could be hiding.

## TL;DR

- **Laptop or prebuilt PC?** The key is probably in the firmware. One PowerShell command shows it.
- **Bought a key online?** It's in your order email.
- **Upgraded from Windows 10, or activated with your Microsoft account?** You may have a **digital license** and no key at all, and that's fine.

## Method 1: the PowerShell command (firmware key)

Most PCs sold with Windows 8 or later have the key stored in the motherboard. To show it:

1. Right-click **Start** and open **Terminal** or **PowerShell**.
2. Paste this and press Enter:

```
(Get-CimInstance -ClassName SoftwareLicensingService).OA3xOriginalProductKey
```

If a 25-character key appears, that's your original key. Copy it somewhere safe.

The old `wmic path softwarelicensingservice get OA3xOriginalProductKey` command does the same thing, but `wmic` is removed by default on newer Windows 11 builds, so use the PowerShell version.

**Blank result?** Your PC either never had a firmware key (common on custom builds) or uses a digital license. Keep reading.

## Method 2: your order email

If you bought a key, it's in the order confirmation. Search your inbox for "product key", the store's name, or "Windows 11". Keys from us arrive by email after payment, so search for **Official Keys Hub**.

## Method 3: the sticker

Older PCs (Windows 7 era) have a **Certificate of Authenticity** sticker on the bottom, the back, or under the battery. Newer PCs usually don't have one, because the key moved into the firmware.

## Method 4: check what you have with slmgr

Run `slmgr /dli` (Win + R, then Enter). It shows the **last 5 characters** of the installed key and the license type. That won't give you the full key, but it helps you match it against an old email or a sticker.

## "I don't have a key at all"

That's normal if you have a **digital license**. It happens when:

- You upgraded from Windows 10 to 11 for free.
- Your PC was activated once and Windows saved the license to Microsoft's servers.
- You linked activation to your Microsoft account.

When you reinstall the **same edition** on the **same PC**, just click *"I don't have a product key"* during setup. Windows reactivates by itself once it's online. Full steps: [How to reinstall Windows without losing your license](/blog/reinstall-windows-without-losing-license), and what a digital license actually is: [Digital license vs product key](/blog/digital-license-vs-product-key).

Changed your motherboard? Sign in with your Microsoft account and use the **Activation troubleshooter** ("I changed hardware on this device recently"). More on that in our [license transfer guide](/blog/transfer-windows-license-new-pc).

## Skip the shady key-finder tools

Plenty of "free key finder" downloads are adware, or worse. You don't need them, because the PowerShell command reads the same firmware key. And no tool can recover a key from a digital license, because there isn't one stored on the PC.

## Still no key? Get a genuine one

If you built the PC yourself, moved to new hardware, or your old license was a work or volume key, you'll need a new license. A genuine **Windows 11 Pro** key activates on Microsoft's servers in seconds, and if you're unsure between Home and Pro, our [key finder tool](/key-finder) tells you in two questions.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I find my Windows 11 product key with CMD?

Open PowerShell and run `(Get-CimInstance -ClassName SoftwareLicensingService).OA3xOriginalProductKey`. It shows the key stored in your PC's firmware, if there is one.

### Why does the command show nothing?

Your PC has no key in its firmware. That's common on custom-built PCs and on PCs activated with a digital license.

### Can I reinstall Windows without a product key?

Yes, if the PC already has a digital license for that edition. Choose "I don't have a product key" during setup and Windows activates automatically once it connects to the internet.

### Can I use my old Windows 10 key for Windows 11?

Usually yes. A genuine Windows 10 Home or Pro key generally activates the same edition of Windows 11.

### Does my product key work on another PC?

A retail key can usually be moved to a new PC after you remove it from the old one. An OEM key built into a laptop's firmware is tied to that device.
