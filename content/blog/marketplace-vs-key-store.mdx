Google "buy Windows 11 key" and the first results are usually marketplaces: **Eneba, Kinguin, G2A**. They look like normal shops, but they work differently from a store like ours, and that difference matters most on the day something goes wrong.

Quick disclosure, since you're on our site: we're a key store, so we have a side here. We'll stick to how each model works and let you decide.

## TL;DR

- **Marketplace:** lots of independent sellers, one website. You're buying from whoever listed that key, and prices are often a bit lower.
- **Key store:** one business sells its own keys and owns the support and refunds.
- **Either way, the rules are the same:** the key should activate on Microsoft's servers, never through an "activator", and you should know exactly who refunds you if it doesn't.

## How marketplaces work

A marketplace is like eBay for game and software keys. Thousands of sellers list keys, the platform shows you the cheapest offers and takes a commission on each sale.

**The good:**

- Very low prices, because sellers compete.
- A huge range of products in one place.

**The catch:**

- **The seller can change.** Two people buying "Windows 11 Pro" on the same day can get keys from two completely different sellers.
- **Quality varies by seller.** Some are excellent, some aren't. Seller ratings help, but a seller can be new or switch where they source keys.
- **Support goes through the platform.** If a key fails, you usually open a dispute and wait for the seller and platform to sort it out. Some marketplaces sell extra buyer protection as a paid add-on at checkout.

## How a key store works

A key store, like Official Keys Hub, sells its own stock. There's no third party in between.

**The good:**

- **One business is responsible.** If a key doesn't activate, you contact the store that sold it and they fix or refund it.
- **Consistent product descriptions.** The store knows exactly what key type it sells (online, phone or bind) and should tell you before you pay.

**The catch:**

- **Smaller range.** A store sells what it stocks, not everything on the internet.
- **Prices aren't always the lowest.** The cheapest marketplace listing can beat a store by a few dollars.

## The 5 questions to ask before buying anywhere

1. **Who exactly is the seller?** On a marketplace, check the individual seller's rating and how long they've been selling, not just the platform.
2. **What type of key is it?** Online, phone or bind key? Retail or OEM? If the listing won't say, that's a red flag. More on key types [here](/blog/online-key-vs-phone-key-vs-bind-key).
3. **Does it activate on Microsoft's servers?** If the seller sends a script or "activator" tool, it isn't a license. It's a crack.
4. **Who refunds you, and how fast?** Look for a clear written refund policy. Ours is a [30-day money-back guarantee](/refund-policy).
5. **Can you pay with buyer protection?** PayPal and cards give you a dispute option. Crypto-only sellers don't.

## So which should you pick?

- **Chasing the absolute lowest price** and comfortable checking seller ratings and opening disputes? A marketplace can work.
- **Want one business that owns the result**, with a clear refund policy and support you can message directly? Use a key store.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Eneba legit for Windows keys?

Eneba is a real, established marketplace. The keys are sold by independent sellers listed on it, though, so quality depends on the seller you buy from. Check that seller's rating and the refund terms before paying.

### Why are marketplace keys so cheap?

Sellers compete on price, and some source keys from regions where licenses cost less, or sell OEM and surplus licenses. A price far below everyone else's is a warning sign.

### What happens if a marketplace key doesn't work?

You usually open a dispute through the platform, which contacts the seller. How fast it's resolved depends on the platform's rules and any buyer protection you bought.

### Is it safer to buy directly from Microsoft?

Microsoft's own store is the most straightforward option, at full retail price. Resellers cost much less; if you use one, pick a seller that tells you the key type and gives a clear refund guarantee.
