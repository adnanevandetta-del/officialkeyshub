When you shop for a Windows or Office license you'll often see the same product listed three ways: **Online Key**, **Phone Key** and **Bind Key**. They unlock the same software. The difference is *how* the key is activated and how easy it is to reinstall later. This guide explains each one in plain English so you can pick the right one in under a minute.

## The short answer

- **Most people with an internet connection:** pick an **Online Key**. It's the cheapest and activates in seconds.
- **The PC is offline, or online activation fails:** pick a **Phone Key**.
- **You reinstall often or want the license tied to your Microsoft account (Office):** pick a **Bind Key**.

## Online Key — activates over the internet

An online key is the standard way Microsoft software activates. You type the 25-character key into **Settings → System → Activation** (Windows) or into any Office app, and Microsoft's activation servers confirm it within a few seconds.

- **Needs:** an internet connection at the moment you activate.
- **Best for:** home and office PCs that are normally online.
- **Example:** [Windows 11 Pro – Online Key](/products/windows-11-pro-online-key).

Once Windows is activated online, Microsoft records a *digital license* for that PC's hardware, so a clean reinstall on the same machine usually reactivates on its own.

## Phone Key — activates by telephone, no internet needed

A phone key is activated through Microsoft's automated phone activation line instead of over the internet. You enter the key, choose phone activation, read out (or type) the **Installation ID** shown on screen, and get back a **Confirmation ID** to enter. It takes about five minutes.

- **Needs:** a phone — the PC itself can stay offline.
- **Best for:** offline or air-gapped machines, workshops, labs, or any PC where online activation returns an error.
- **Example:** [Windows 11 Pro – Phone Key](/products/windows-11-pro-phone-key).

Phone keys cost a little more because they're more flexible about *where* the PC is. If you get stuck, our [activation guide](/activation-guide) walks through each screen.

## Bind Key — linked to your Microsoft account

A bind key (common for Office) is redeemed once and then attached to your **Microsoft account**. After that, you reinstall Office by signing in at account.microsoft.com and downloading it again — no need to keep the key itself safe.

- **Needs:** a Microsoft account (free) and an internet connection.
- **Best for:** people who change PCs, reinstall Windows often, or just don't want to lose track of a product key.
- **Example:** [Office 2024 Pro Plus – Bind Key](/products/office-2024-pro-plus-bind-key).

## Is one "more genuine" than the others?

No. All three are genuine Microsoft licenses for one PC — they activate the same edition with the same features. The label only describes the activation route. Whichever you choose, it should activate directly with Microsoft, never through third-party "activator" tools.

## Which one should you buy?

1. **Is the PC online?** If yes, an **Online Key** is the simplest and cheapest choice.
2. **No internet on that PC, or online activation keeps failing?** Choose a **Phone Key**.
3. **Buying Office and want painless reinstalls?** Choose a **Bind Key**.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use an online key without internet?

No. An online key needs an internet connection at the moment you activate. If the PC will stay offline, choose a phone key instead.

### Is phone activation free?

Yes. Microsoft's phone activation line doesn't charge for activation. You only need the Installation ID shown on your screen.

### Can I move a bind key to a new PC?

A bind key lives on your Microsoft account, so you can sign in and install Office on your new PC. It's licensed for one PC at a time, so remove it from the old one first. See our guide on [transferring a license to a new PC](/blog/transfer-windows-license-new-pc).
