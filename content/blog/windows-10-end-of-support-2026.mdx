Microsoft ended free mainstream support for Windows 10 on **14 October 2025**. If you're still running it in 2026, your PC keeps working — but it no longer receives the free security updates that quietly protect you every month. This guide explains what that actually means and walks through your realistic options, from doing nothing to moving to Windows 11 for the price of a coffee-and-lunch.

## What "end of support" really means

End of support does **not** mean Windows 10 stops working overnight. Your apps still run and your files are all still there. What changes is behind the scenes:

- **No more free security patches.** New vulnerabilities discovered after the cutoff won't be fixed on the free tier.
- **No feature or quality updates.** The OS is frozen in place.
- **Shrinking app support.** Over time, browsers and apps drop testing on Windows 10.

In short: it's still usable, but every month it becomes a slightly larger target.

## Is Windows 10 still safe to use?

For a short while, with good habits, yes — but the risk grows over time. If you stay on Windows 10, at minimum keep a reputable antivirus running, use a modern browser that still updates, and avoid it for sensitive tasks like banking on an unmaintained machine. This is a stopgap, not a plan.

## Your options in 2026

### Option 1 — Upgrade to Windows 11 (recommended)

If your PC meets the requirements, upgrading to Windows 11 is the cleanest fix: you're back on a fully supported, patched operating system. You can buy a genuine [Windows 11 Pro](/products/windows-11-pro) or Windows 11 Home license for a fraction of retail and activate it directly with Microsoft.

### Option 2 — Buy a new PC

The simplest route if your hardware is old, but also the most expensive. Most people don't need it — a license upgrade is usually enough.

### Option 3 — Pay Microsoft for Extended Security Updates (ESU)

Microsoft offers paid ESU to keep receiving security fixes for a limited time. It buys you breathing room but costs money each year and is a temporary bridge, not a destination.

### Option 4 — Do nothing

Free, but the least safe. Fine for an offline machine; risky for anything connected to the internet.

## Can your PC run Windows 11?

Windows 11 needs a compatible 64-bit CPU, 4 GB+ RAM, 64 GB+ storage, Secure Boot and TPM 2.0. Open **Settings → Windows Update** and run Microsoft's PC Health Check, or check **Settings → Privacy & security → Device security** for TPM. Many PCs from the last several years qualify — sometimes TPM just needs enabling in the BIOS.
